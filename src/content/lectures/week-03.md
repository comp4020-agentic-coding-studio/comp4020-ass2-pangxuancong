The reroll is the only choice the mode hands back before the game starts, and
it is the one most often spent badly. Not because players reroll champions they
should keep — sometimes they should reroll — but because the button is pressed
before anybody has worked out what is being bought and what is being paid.

## What the button actually costs

Three prices, and only the first is visible.

**A reroll is spent currency.** It accumulates slowly, across games. Using one
now is a decision not to have it in some future game where the roll is worse.
The cost is not paid in this game at all, which is exactly why it is
underweighted.

**A reroll discards a known quantity for an unknown one.** You are trading a
champion you can evaluate for a draw from a distribution. If the champion in
hand is already acceptable, most of that distribution is not an improvement.

**A reroll changes the team, not just you.** Whatever you were contributing —
the only frontline, the only waveclear, the second engage — leaves with the
champion you discarded. Week 6 takes this up properly; the point here is that
the reroll is evaluated as a personal decision and is not one.

## The choice, priced

A worked case. You are given **Azir**. Your team is filling in around you:
Azir, Maokai, Ashe, Seraphine, Renekton.

| option | what you get | what you pay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| keep Azir | a champion with a very high ceiling and a very high floor-to-ceiling gap | whatever the gap is, in your hands |
| reroll | a draw from the roster | the currency, the known quantity, and Azir's role in the composition |
| trade | a teammate's champion, if one is offered | nothing, except that you now owe the read |

The interesting variable is not on this table, and it is the one that decides
the answer: **how well do you play Azir?**

A champion's strength is a property of the champion. Its value to you is a
property of the pairing. A strong champion played at sixty per cent of its
requirement is frequently worse than a modest champion played at full. The
population data cannot see this, because it is averaged over players who are
not you.

## The trap: maximising

The failure mode has a name outside this subject. A **maximiser** searches for
the best available option; a **satisficer** takes the first option above a
threshold and stops. In a setting where search is free, maximising wins. In one
where search costs something and the distribution is wide, it usually does not.

Rerolling is search with a price. The player who rerolls an acceptable champion
because a better one exists somewhere in the pool is paying a real cost for an
expected improvement that is, most of the time, small — and is sometimes
negative, because the roster contains worse outcomes than the one they
discarded.

The discipline is to set the threshold *before* seeing the champion. Something
like: *I keep anything I can play competently that covers a function my team
needs.* Then apply it. A threshold invented after the roll is not a threshold,
it is a justification.

## When rerolling is right

Three cases, and they are narrower than they feel:

1. **You genuinely cannot play it.** Not "I would prefer something else" —
   cannot. Self-knowledge, honestly applied, is the strongest reason on the
   list.
2. **The champion is functionally redundant** and the team has an obvious hole
   your reroll could plausibly fill.
3. **You are holding several rerolls near the cap**, in which case the future
   cost of using one has already been paid.

Note what is not on the list: the champion having a poor win rate.

## The counterargument

Some champions really are unplayable in this mode, and pretending otherwise in
the name of decision discipline is its own kind of error. A melee champion with
no gap-closer, dropped into a bridge full of ranged poke, is not a
satisficing problem. It is a bad hand, and the reroll exists for it.

The argument here is not *never reroll*. It is that the button is pressed
reflexively, on champions that clear the bar, by players who have not stated
the bar. The correction is not to reroll less. It is to be able to say why.

## Takeaway

The reroll is an asset with a price, and it buys a draw rather than an outcome.
Spend it against a threshold you set before you looked.
