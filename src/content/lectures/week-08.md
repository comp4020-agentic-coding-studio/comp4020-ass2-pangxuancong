You cannot decline the fight. Two teams occupy one bridge and disengaging buys
seconds rather than minutes, so the question *should we fight* is not really
available. Contact is coming.

What is available is **what will be true when it arrives**. That is the entire
lever this week, and it is larger than it sounds.

## The state that decides it

A fight is not decided by execution as often as players believe. It is decided
by a state, and the state is legible in advance. Six variables:

| variable | the question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| ultimates | how many of theirs are up, how many of ours |
| key cooldowns | whether the tool that makes their engage work is available |
| health | who can be opened on, and who cannot participate |
| summoner spells | whether the target you plan to kill can leave |
| numbers | whether anybody is dead or walking back |
| ordering | week 7's question, evaluated right now |

A team that fights with four of these in its favour usually wins regardless of
mechanical quality. A team that fights with four against usually loses
regardless. The band in which execution decides the outcome is narrower than
its share of post-game conversation.

## Two clocks

The reason this is a decision at all, rather than an observation, is that the
six variables do not move together.

**Some regenerate.** Cooldowns, ultimates, health, summoner spells — all
return on their own, at rates you can estimate.

**Some do not.** Numbers and ordering only change because somebody does
something.

Forced contact means the fight will happen. It does not mean it happens *now*.
Whoever is better at running the clock on the regenerating variables arrives at
contact with more of them favourable, and this is most of what strong play in
this mode consists of: not winning fights, but arranging to be the team whose
ultimates are up.

## A case

Your **Riven** and **Zilean** against their **Neeko** and **Bard**.

Their whole engage runs through Neeko's ultimate. It has a long cooldown and it
is visible when it is spent. From the moment it goes, there is a window in
which the enemy team's ability to open a fight is materially reduced, and in
which they know it.

Two teams read this window differently. One notices and does nothing, because
the fight had not started and nobody thought to start one. The other treats the
window as the only period in which contact is on favourable terms, and moves
before it closes — accepting a fight it would refuse ten seconds later.

Nothing in the second team's play was mechanically better. They fought in a
different state.

Zilean's presence changes the calculation again, because a resurrection is a
regenerating variable with an unusually long clock. A fight taken with it
available and a fight taken without it are different fights, and the
difference is knowable to both teams.

## Why teams get this wrong

Three reasons worth naming.

**The regenerating variables are invisible unless you look.** Nothing displays
the enemy's ultimate availability. It has to be tracked, and tracking is
effort spent during a period when nothing appears to be happening.

**Doing nothing feels like not deciding.** Waiting through a bad window is a
decision, and it is an unrewarding one, because the payoff arrives later and is
never attributed to it.

**Post-game evidence points the other way.** The fight that was lost looks like
a fight that was misplayed, because the misplay is the visible part. The
condition that doomed it was set thirty seconds earlier by nobody in
particular.

## The counterargument

Waiting for a good state is not free, and the strongest objection is that it is
not always available to wait. A team that is losing the wave, or being sieged,
does not get to choose the moment; the alternative to a bad fight is sometimes
a worse position. Perfect condition-management is a luxury of teams that are
not under pressure.

That is correct, and it sharpens the claim rather than refuting it. The point
is not that you can always fight on good terms. It is that most teams do not
know what terms they are fighting on, and a team that at least knows can tell
the difference between a fight it chose badly and a fight it had to take.

## Takeaway

You cannot refuse contact. You can arrive at it with your ultimates up, and
that is the same decision wearing different clothes.
